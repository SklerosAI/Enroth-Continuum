# MM6 monster effect seams: preference, special attack and loot (F2)

Status: **retained-data, owned-guide and source audit, 2026-11-05**. This packet extends [combat semantics](MM6_COMBAT_SEMANTICS_F2.md) and the [monster guide catalog](MM6_MONSTER_GUIDE_SEMANTICS_F2.md). It does not read the original installation, run MM6, establish original executable behavior, or close F2. `scripts/mm6_probe_monster_effect_seams.py` joins the already ignored 173-row combat, parser and guide ledgers; it writes exact per-ID values, guide pages, hashes and source-risk flags only to ignored `local-game-data/mm6/research-2026-11-05/monster-effect-seams-private.json`.

## All-ID evidence and confidence

The probe confirms ordered coverage for **173/173** retained monster IDs, a shared retained-table hash across three prior reports, and direct guide page-image identity for the 171 catalog variants. The two tail IDs remain outside the printed catalog. It joins attack preferences, AI, special attacks, inherited fixed-drop family, active parser flags and private page locators to every ID. That is a complete **source-risk register**, not a complete effect rulebook: the guide has not supplied original executable probabilities, damage and resistance formulas, faction/target order, or generated-item code.

The earlier parser inventory remains the exhaustive label baseline: 49 of 212 nonzero attack-type cells fall to physical, 12 Energy cells map to Earth, 46 of 82 active missile cells map to none, 13 of 55 monster spell cells map to none, and 11 of 93 active special-attack cells map to none. Six MM6 resistance columns are loaded into ten MM7 channels. These source diagnoses are high-confidence; intended gameplay remains to reconcile at F3 and implement/test at F4/F5.

## Broader guide/source tensions retained for F3

The same owned guide page describes four AI types, a damage-dependent retreat order, secondary-attack recovery, and preference behavior. The retained table has 17 Wimp, 30 Normal, 83 Aggressive and 43 Suicidal rows. The current `Actor.cpp` Wimp path flees while hostile without a damage check, while its Normal/Aggressive paths use source-specific remaining-HP thresholds. The guide is a test hypothesis, not proof of the original executable: F3 must reconcile those thresholds, party/monster hostility, target weighting and recovery units before F4. The current `HostilityTable::InitializeMm6` sets all monster-to-monster relations friendly as a temporary policy; it is not a proven MM6 faction rule.

Energy, Magic and condition resistance are likewise unresolved. `Monsters.cpp` aliases `Ener` to Earth and fans the MM6 Magic column into five MM7 schools, while `Actor::CalcMagicalDamageToActor` has no Energy case. The six retained resistance channels and `Imm` markers need original formula evidence; a direct guide category cannot supply a numeric damage or condition check. Random treasure category labels parse lexically but still route through MM7 item kinds/generation. The 12 fixed-drop collision is separate from those random treasure cells.

- **F4 offline engine work:** separate MM6 target-preference, multi-victim, condition, AI, resistance and loot policies from MM7. Exercise all active label classes, including the 11 unknown special labels, and verify no active label silently falls back. Test a mixed-sex party, each numerical target count, eligible/empty weapon-break inventory, all four inherited fixed-drop family collisions, gold/random/fixed item combinations, Immunity and Energy, and round-trip actor/corpse state. Preserve MM7 behavior.
- **F5 graphical play:** use private saves for representative encounters with visible before/after targeting, multiple victims, special effects, damage/resistance, projected sprites and loot. Check changed equipment/conditions, save/quit/load and map re-entry for persistent loot and actor state. A parser count or title screen is not gameplay evidence.
