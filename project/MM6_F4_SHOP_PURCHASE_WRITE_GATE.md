# MM6 shop purchase-write and record gate — F4

Status, 2026-11-06: the accepted F3-matched original executable proves physical stock-record identity and the successful purchase mutation for the first shop bank, second shop bank and guild. Detached MM6-only helpers model that mutation and preserve the contiguous raw physical region in an isolated `SHOP` section. The section is **not** part of the required known-save manifest; no live stock owner or service writer is added. Normal MM6 SaveLoad and house services remain guarded.

## What the original trace establishes

- The two shop menu branches select distinct banks. The selected slot and display scan use the current original MM6 house row and a 12-position row of 28-byte records. The first 32-bit word is an item ID; zero suppresses a displayed slot.
- The spell-guild generator and display path use the same 12-position, 28-byte shape. The final active shop row's second bank and the first active guild row address the same twelve physical records. The existing [physical index helper](../src/Engine/MM6/MM6ShopStockIdentity.h) expresses this alias and rejects foreign titles and out-of-range keys.
- The successful purchase paths compute selected-record addresses from the same physical stock region. The first shop bank joins the second bank's success path after its own pointer calculation; the guild has a corresponding success path. Each copies seven 32-bit words (the full 28-byte record) to inventory, then zeroes source bytes **0–24**, leaving bytes **25–27** as they were. The zero-ID display check alone did not establish this; the purchase write now does. Failed gold or inventory checks reach no source clear on these paths.
- The original raw Party save/read span includes the stock region. That is persistence of the original process memory, not a complete contract for this fork's live keyed owner.

## New-game clearing confirms one physical region

A further read-only check of the accepted F3-matched executable found the new-game initializer clearing three consecutive runs of complete 28-byte records: 564 records from the first shop-bank origin, another 564 from the second-bank origin, and 312 from the following guild origin. Those counts cover physical rows **0–46**, **47–93**, and **94–119** respectively, at twelve records per row. The last active shop row (row 47) in the second bank addresses physical row 94; the first active guild row (row 119) also addresses physical row 94. Both retained house rows have active references, and their respective menu paths read nonzero item IDs. This confirms that the shared edge is inside one cleared physical region, not two independently initialized arrays. It does not establish whether a player can observe both services' nonzero stock before refresh.

The initializer also explains why three disjoint logical array counts are misleading: the 47th active shop row crosses into the next clearing run, and the first active guild row begins at the same physical row. A future live owner must use a single physical index and define the shared service lifetime explicitly. The detached [purchase helper](../src/Engine/MM6/MM6ShopStockPurchase.h) copies a complete record and applies the original partial clear only after an MM6 caller has accepted the sale; it is not wired into a service or save transaction.

## Detached physical stock shape

The [MM6 stock fields](../src/Engine/MM6/MM6ShopStockFields.h) hold exactly **120 × 12** physical 28-byte records. They preserve all raw bytes, including inactive/sentinel rows and the three bytes left after a successful purchase. `SHOP` v1 encodes a one-byte version followed by those 40,320 bytes, for an exact 40,321-byte payload. It requires an explicit ready MM6 owner. Decode validates the whole `.ec6` envelope, section tag, required flag, version and length before replacing a detached destination; a malformed final byte leaves the prior owner untouched. This is a lossless representation of the proved original stock region, without interpreting item fields or creating separate shop/guild copies.

The generic container recognizes the `SHOP` tag so isolated section probes can reject malformed required data. `KnownSaveFields` still requires exactly eighteen sections and rejects `SHOP` as an extra section. The current fork has no MM6 service writer or capture/stage adapter from live `Party` stock. This shape is **not a durable gameplay save** and does not release the normal SaveLoad guard.

## Independent editor cross-check and mismatch

The pinned [MM6 `PartyBin.java` research-editor implementation](https://github.com/angeld29/mm8leveleditor/blob/bf3b8c4a86100635a5fc6ff53fac6e4e0af5cb53/src/org/gamenet/application/mm8leveleditor/data/mm6/fileFormat/PartyBin.java) reads two 47-row shop lists followed by a separate 22-row guild list of `ShopContents`. Relative to the original executable's first-shop-bank origin, those consecutive lists start at physical row indices 1, 48 and 95. The original guild address formula starts its first active row at physical index 94, which is also the last shop row of bank two. Thus the editor's guild list starts **one 12-slot row later** than the original first active guild address. The original clearing runs now independently confirm physical row 94 belongs to its guild initialization range. The editor itself treats its counts as uncertain. Its [28-byte item interpretation](https://github.com/angeld29/mm8leveleditor/blob/bf3b8c4a86100635a5fc6ff53fac6e4e0af5cb53/src/org/gamenet/application/mm8leveleditor/data/mm6/ContainedItem.java) is useful for field hypotheses, but cannot override the original address or purchase writes. The arithmetic mismatch is an inference from the two sources, not a claim that a native save has been observed with nonzero shared slots.

## Codec decision and exact next proof

The detached raw codec proves that bytes can be retained without splitting the physical alias; it does not determine which shared record a service owns over refresh or capture changed live stock from this fork. A codec restricted to the item-ID word would discard the other 24 bytes, including the three bytes retained after sale. A pair of disjoint 47-row shop arrays plus 22-row guild array would assign two logical authorities at the shared edge and shift the guild layout. `SCLK` remains the only commerce section in the required known-save manifest; it owns the separately proved refresh deadlines.

The remaining prerequisite is to compare a nonzero owner-local native `party.bin` record with this physical layout and trace the next stock read across a sale/refresh at the shared edge. Then define the live MM6-keyed owner lifecycle and deterministic alias policy, connect MM6 generation/purchase paths to it, and test a nonzero sale/refresh round-trip plus duplicate-alias and late-stage-failure rejection on detached copies. Keep ban and bounty clocks/targets separate until their own row and lifetime evidence exists.

The new stock-field and unit translation units passed arm64 syntax compilation, and a standalone native codec probe passed nonzero alias/tail round-trip and malformed-load nonmutation. Linked stock-section execution remains for integration. No native save, proprietary bytes or live house service were added to Git or enabled.
