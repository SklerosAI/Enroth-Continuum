# MM6 monster projectile source gap

Status: F4 title-specific source labels and launch gate, 2026-11-06. MM6 projectile sprites and impacts remain unverified.

The F3-matched retained `monsters.txt` has 82 active nonempty projectile cells: 55 primary and 27 secondary. The inherited `ParseMissleAttackType` recognized 36 of them and returned `MONSTER_PROJECTILE_NONE` for 46: 17 Electricity, ten Cold, six Poison, four Magic, three Rock, three Dagger and three flaming-arrow abbreviations. `NONE` is also the engine's melee/no-projectile sentinel, so that fallback changed attack selection before projectile rendering or damage. The [F2 combat audit](MM6_COMBAT_SEMANTICS_F2.md) recorded the same 46-cell gap.

The MM6 loader now maps all ten active source labels to distinct title-specific projectile IDs; an unknown active label rejects table loading, and zero-chance secondary placeholders remain `NONE`. The offline native test loaded all 173 monster rows and counted each of the 82 active cells by label. MM6 AI now sees a non-`NONE` type for those ranged attacks, and `AI_RangedAttack` rejects MM6 launch before inherited sprite/object creation. MM7 retains its original parser and launch path. This fixes source and ranged-choice classification while deliberately keeping projectile gameplay closed.

Some labels suggest familiar projectiles, but the current enum and sprite lookup have no explicit MM6 Magic, Rock or Dagger contract. Mapping source labels to inherited MM7 projectile IDs would guess their sprites, travel, impact and resistance behavior. The monster damage and spell-effect gates do not establish projectile visuals or effects.

The retained MM6 `dobjlist.bin` supplies a direct object-row join for nine of the ten labels: Arrow, Flaming Arrow, Fire, Electricity, Cold, Poison, Energy, Magic and Rock. A title-specific lookup now returns their MM6 object IDs, and a native test decoded all 232 retained object rows plus 6,455 `dsft.bin` sprite frames. Each mapped object points to its corresponding named `msl01`–`msl09` first frame with a nonempty texture name. Seven of the missile rows also have adjacent, zero-speed explosion objects (`mslX03`–`mslX09`). A separate title-specific lookup retains these impact object IDs while leaving Arrow, Flaming Arrow and Dagger unresolved. The [offline frame checker](../scripts/mm6_f4_check_projectile_frames.py) walks all 85 frames across the nine missile and seven impact sequences and matches every referenced texture prefix to the retained `SPRITES.entries` archive index. The Dagger label has no proved projectile row; `dsft.bin` has a `knifid` sprite family and ordinary dagger item rows exist, but neither proves which object or motion a monster throw uses. The lookup is not wired into launch, and it does not prove animation, impact, sound or damage.

The remaining runtime call chain is title-sensitive beyond object lookup. `Actor::AI_RangedAttack` currently asks the inherited `spriteForMonsterProjectile` for an MM7 object, then creates a `SpriteObject` and calls `pIndoor->GetSector` unconditionally. `processSpellImpact` dispatches impacts by the inherited `SpriteId` switch; `isMonsterProjectileSprite` in the character damage path also uses MM7 IDs. Raw IDs collide: MM6 Arrow is object 500, while the inherited enum calls 500 Air Bolt; MM6 Fire is 520, while that enum calls 520 Body Bolt. A future launch adapter must use game title at launch, indoor/outdoor sector setup, impact dispatch and damage/shield classification. The seven verified explosion rows are resource joins only; no inherited impact branch is safe to reuse by numeric equality.

Next F4 work: resolve Dagger and arrow impacts, decode or render the candidate sequences, and verify timing, octants and in-game use; then implement launch, travel, impact and damage through a title-specific adapter. F5 must inspect representative Electricity, Cold, Poison, Magic, Rock, Dagger and flaming-arrow shots, including sound and save/re-entry. MM7's parser and projectile path should remain independent.
